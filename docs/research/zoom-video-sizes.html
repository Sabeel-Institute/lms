All video is H.264 at 1920×1080 and 25 frames a second (1920×1200 on 17 September, which followed the shared screen's shape). Audio inside the files runs at 66 to 76 kbps. Sizes come from Zoom's recording list, and resolutions from reading each file's header. No video was downloaded.

Why the sizes differ

Size follows movement, not length

A video file stores what changes from one frame to the next. A still slide costs almost nothing after its first frame. A moving camera changes every part of the picture, all the time, and every change has to be stored.

Slides only: steady

48 to 65 MB per hour in every class. Slides change a few times a minute and nothing else moves.

Slides + speaker: depends on the camera

85 to 126 MB per hour on four classes, and 370 MB per hour on 1 October. Same course, same settings, same length as 24 September. The file headers cannot say what moved more that day, only that something did.

A whiteboard camera is the hard case

A camera filming a board sees the teacher move, the light shift and its own sensor noise. If it is hand-held or keeps moving, all of that is stored. The cost calculator assumes 1 GB per hour for that reason.

Recording the whiteboard

Put the camera on a tripod

A phone on a tripod is enough. A camera that stays still, framed on the board, keeps the writing readable for students and keeps the file small, because most of the picture stays the same from frame to frame.

  • Frame the board and leave it. The whole board in view, close enough to read, and no panning or zooming during class.
  • Keep the light steady. Avoid pointing the camera at a window or a bright light behind the board. Changing light makes the camera adjust and the whole picture shift.
  • Getting it into the recording. A phone can join the class as its own participant with its microphone muted and its camera spotlighted. A second camera plugged into the teacher's computer can be shared as content (Share, then Advanced, then Content from 2nd Camera), so the board fills the shared-screen file.

What a steady camera should come to

Probably in the range the slide classes already show: 0.1 to 0.4 GB per hour at 1080p, against about 1 GB per hour for a moving camera. This is an estimate. One test class with a tripod camera will give the real figure.

After the class

Converting to 720p roughly halves it again

720p has 44% of the pixels of 1080p, and a board written in marker reads well at that size. The app would keep a 720p copy of each class and serve that to students. A board also needs fewer than 25 frames a second, and a lower frame rate saves more.

  • Expect about half the size from 720p alone, and more with a lower frame rate. In a test on deliberately noisy 1080p video, converting to 720p at 10 frames a second made the file 78% smaller.
  • It can run on Firebase. A background function with 8 processors and 8 GB of memory should convert a 3-hour class in about 8 to 15 minutes, inside its 30-minute limit. The memory needed is small.
  • The computing is likely free. A month of Quran Immersion would use about a tenth of the free allowance.
What it means for cost

Smaller files stretch the free tier

Firebase gives 5 GB of storage and 100 GB of downloads a month free, then charges $0.020 per GB a month to store and $0.12 per GB downloaded.
