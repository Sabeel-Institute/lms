Class Recordings app · Proposal, second draft · 12 October 2026

Proposal · for review before building

Assignments, as part of the class

Teachers post assignments to the classes they teach. Students hand in a note and their files, and teachers grade the work and return it. This draft builds that into the app rather than beside it. Students get one To do list for everything they owe. Teachers find grading in their Today list and on the course page. The class workbook carries attendance, listening and assignments together.

Students
  • Listening becomes To do: required listening and assignments in one list, soonest first.
  • An assignment opens on its instructions and files. Below them, a student writes a note, attaches files or takes a photo, and hands in.
  • A grade and feedback arrive when the teacher returns the work, with a notification.
  • Each class page shows their attendance, listening and assignments.
Teachers
  • Post from the course page: formatted instructions, files, a due date and how it is graded.
  • Work waiting for a grade appears on Today.
  • Grade on a phone or a laptop, one student after the next, and return the work together or one at a time.
  • Give one student more time, or ask for another try.
  • See who has handed in, who is late and who has nothing in, per assignment and per student.
Admins
  • Everything a teacher can do, in every course.
  • Workbooks gain assignments and grades beside attendance and listening.
  • Posting, editing, returning, and marking work not required are all in the audit history.
  • Only an admin can delete an assignment that students have handed work in to.
Three things needed your yes — all approved 2026-10-13
  • The app's scope widens. The product brief says it "is not a general LMS" and lists file attachments as out of scope. With assignments it covers three things a class needs: attendance, required listening, and assignments with grades. It is still not a general LMS: no course grades, quizzes, discussion boards or video.
  • Students' first tab is renamed from Listening to To do. This is the most visible change for students already using the app. The manual and the release notes will say so.
  • Three written rules change. Enrolment gives a student the class's assignments, though still never a recording. A teacher may delete an assignment no student has started, a third deletion left to managers. And the brief's "three messages, no more" becomes six notification switches.

Everything else is decided in this draft, with the reason for each choice in section 10. Any of them can be changed. The app's name, Class Recordings, is left alone here (section 8).

Since the first draft
  • Listening and assignments share one To do list. The first draft gave assignments their own tab, which left students two places to check.
  • Due dates are whole days, like listen-by dates. Due soon means the same week, and the reminder comes at 7 AM on the due date. Late work is accepted until a second date, so every open assignment has a day it closes.
  • Added: assignments on the course page and in Today, a Grades report and gradebook, workbook sheets, a staff view of one student in one course, more time for one student, another try, and the rules for joining late, leaving, archiving, editing and deleting.
  • The first draft's ten questions are settled, each with its reason.
1

One class, three records

A class already keeps two records about each student: attendance and required listening. Assignments add a third. All three appear on the same screens, but each follows its own rules.

As now

Attendance

Staff take the register for each session and mark every student present, absent or excused.

One per session · staff mark it

excused
As now

Required listening

Only a student marked excused gets that session's recording, and must listen to it by its listen-by date.

One per session · excused students only

separate
New

Assignments

Posted by a teacher to the whole class, with a due date of its own. Not tied to a session, a register mark or a recording.

Any number per course · every enrolled student

What keeps them apart

  • Attendance has no bearing on assignments. Present, absent or excused, a student gets every assignment in the class.
  • An assignment never opens a recording, and a recording never creates an assignment.
  • A teacher can post on any day, whether or not there was a session that week.

What they share

  • Dates work the same way: a due date is a whole day in institute time, as a listen-by date is.
  • The same words: Due soon, Upcoming, Missed. "Hand in by" sits where "Listen by" does.
  • The same 7 AM reminder on the last day, and the same rule that nothing is ever set already overdue.

2

Students

Students get no new tab. Their first tab, Listening, becomes To do and holds both kinds of work. Each class page gains an Assignments tab beside its sessions. The mockups follow one student, Aminah Bello, through a Monday in October. She takes two classes this term and finished a third in the spring.

To do: everything she owes, in one list

The list works as the Listening list does now. The most urgent open item is drawn large at the top. Everything else is grouped beneath it, and each row says what to do and by when: Listen by for a recording, Hand in by for an assignment.

The order, top to bottom

  1. Next up: the nearest date still ahead, of either kind. For late work that is the last day it is accepted.
  2. Past due: assignments whose due date has passed and that the teacher still accepts, each with the day it closes. These are the only items that are late but can still be done.
  3. Due soon: due today or in the next seven days.
  4. Upcoming: due later.
  5. Missed and Archived: closed without being done. Kept quietly as the record.
  6. Done: the last two weeks of recordings completed and assignments handed in or returned, newest first. Everything older is on each class page.

Work marked not required leaves To do and stays on the class page. With nothing open, the list says: "Nothing to do right now. New recordings and assignments will appear here."

Why the nearest date leads

Everything open has a day it closes: a recording on its listen-by date, an assignment on its due date or, if late work is accepted, on the last day it is. The large card shows the nearest of those days, whichever kind it is. Work that is already late sits directly under it, so it is never below work that is not yet due.

What stays exactly as it is

Recordings open the player and are completed as they are now. The rule for which recordings appear is untouched: only those she was excused for.

An assignment

Tapping an assignment opens a single screen: the teacher's instructions and files at the top, her own work underneath. Everything she does there is saved as she goes. The teacher sees nothing until she presses Hand in.

The same screen in three other states, from her other classes.

Handing in

  • A note, plain text, and up to ten files. Either can be empty, but not both.
  • Take a photo opens the camera, on Android and in a phone's browser. Photos are shrunk on the phone before they upload.
  • Handing in needs a connection. If the signal drops, the note and the files already uploaded are kept; a file that was still uploading has to be added again.

Late, closed, and changing your mind

  • Work handed in after the due date is marked late, to the teacher and to her.
  • Take back is there until the due date, while the work has not been returned. After the due date, taking work back would make it late, so the button goes. A teacher who has started grading keeps the draft grade, and sees the new version when it comes back.
  • Once returned, the work is final unless the teacher asks for another try.

Her classes, and one class

The class page is her record in one class. It keeps the sessions she sees today: each register mark, and whether the recording was required. It adds a second tab for that class's assignments. Unlike the session rows, each assignment opens.

No average or overall grade is shown to students. The institute has no rule for combining assignments into a course grade, and a number the app invented would read as an official one.

What tells her

Four new messages under three new switches, in the same voice as the two she gets now. The last-day reminder comes at 7 AM, as the listening one does; the others arrive when the teacher acts. A moved due date shares the switch for new assignments. Every switch is on by default.

3

Teachers

A teacher here is anyone who manages the course: its named managers, and every admin. They see assignments for exactly the courses they see today, and post, grade and return them there. Nothing new is added to the tab bar. Grading reaches a teacher through Today, and everything else lives on the course page.

These mockups are from Thursday 15 October, two days after Memorize the first hikma was due.

Today: grading joins the queue

Today already lists every piece of work waiting on a teacher, each row a link straight to where it is done. Work to grade becomes one more group at the end, with one row per assignment, including work handed in early. Each row is derived from live data, like every other row, so it disappears when the work is done. Grading rows appear only for courses the reader manages by name. An admin's Today covers every running course, and other teachers' grading would bury the registers.

Why grading sits last and adds nothing to the badge

The badge on Today counts only what is keeping students away from a recording right now: a register not taken, or a recording withdrawn before its date. Ungraded work keeps nobody from anything. If it were counted, the badge would almost never be zero, and a badge that is never zero gets ignored.

No message per hand-in

A class of twenty would send a teacher twenty messages for one assignment. Today carries the count instead. Teachers keep their single existing notification, about registers not taken.

The course page, and its assignments

The course page gains a second main button beside Sessions, and a second report beside Attendance report. Its counts card gains one line. A course's assignments are listed in one place: drafts first, then the rest with the latest due date first, as sessions are listed by meeting date.

Writing an assignment

One form on a phone or the website: a title, formatted instructions, files, a due date, how it is graded, and whether late work is accepted. A teacher can post it at once or save it as a draft. A draft is seen by the course's other teachers and by no student.

Instructions

The same five controls as the institute's Kanban app: bold, italic, a bulleted list, a numbered list, a link. Nothing else to learn, and nothing a phone cannot show. Arabic paragraphs should run right to left on their own; that needs testing on Android and the web before it ships.

Files

Worksheets, scans, slides, pictures, audio. Up to ten, 25 MB each, and a teacher's audio at most 15 minutes. Video is refused, as it is everywhere in the app.

Hand in by, and late work

The due date is required, a date rather than a time, and never in the past. It prefills to a week from today. Late work is accepted until a second date, a week after the due date unless the teacher changes it. So every open assignment has a day it closes.

Graded as

Points, out of any whole number (halves allowed when grading), or Satisfactory / needs work. Feedback is optional either way. The choice cannot change once a grade has been saved.

Who gets it

Everyone in the course when it is posted, and anyone who joins by its due date. The sentence above the button says how many, and who is told.

Following one assignment

An assignment's page answers one question: where is everyone? Its counts and filters are laid out like the listening progress page's, so a teacher who knows one can read the other. The roster is sorted by name, as the course roster is.

The counts

Expected is everyone it went to, minus anyone marked not required. Past due counts students with nothing in while late work is still accepted; Missed, those whose time ran out. Before the due date, the Past due tile reads Not in yet.

The header

Grade walks through the work still to grade. Edit changes the instructions, files and dates, and holds Stop accepting work and, where allowed, Delete. The Instructions tab shows the assignment exactly as students see it.

One student at a time

From a student's row a teacher can give that student more time, or mark the work not required. Both need a reason, and the student sees it, as with a listening override.

Grading without a hand-in

Work done on paper or recited in class can be graded at any time. The record shows it was graded without a hand-in.

Grading

Grading is one screen per student. The work sits on top: the note, pictures large enough to read handwriting, and audio that plays in the app with the same speed control as a recording. The grade and optional feedback sit underneath. Everything saves as it is typed. A teacher moves on with Next and returns the class's work together at the end, or returns one piece at once.

4

Tracking and reports

Assignments are reported the way attendance already is, by assignment and by student. The course's assignment list is the view by assignment. The Grades report is the view by student, and a gradebook on a wide screen. A student's page in a course shows everything for that student. The workbook carries the same figures, with the same definitions on its last tab.

Grades: each student, across every assignment

The Grades button on the course page opens it. On a phone it is a list of cards, one per student, like the attendance report's By student view. On a wide screen it becomes a gradebook: one row per student, one column per assignment.

One student, one class

Tapping a student on the roster already opens their required listening in that course. That screen becomes the student's record in the course, with a tab for listening and a tab for assignments. The Assignments tab lists what the student sees on their class page, with staff figures beside it: past-due and missed work, grades still in draft, and an average of returned grades. The Listening tab is the existing screen, unchanged.

The attendance report stays as it is

It reports the register and catch-up listening, which are about sessions. Assignments get their own report rather than a third line on every card there. The two reports are siblings on the course page, and the workbook brings them together.

Admins across courses

An admin sees every course's Grades report. A manager sees only theirs. The scope is the same as every other report in the app.

What is not counted

Assignments not yet due are never counted against anyone. Work marked not required counts nowhere, and nor does work closed because its course was archived. Students who left the course stay in the report, flagged no longer enrolled, so the totals still add up, as the attendance report does now.

The workbook

Export workbook on the course page and on a student's page gains assignments beside attendance and listening. The new sheets follow the existing ones: a grid like Register and Listening, and a detail sheet like Detail.

Hand-in rate is handed in ÷ (handed in + missed), the counterpart of the catch-up rate. Like that rate, it holds nothing still open against anyone: past-due work the teacher still accepts counts neither way.

The audit history

Every change a teacher makes to an assignment or a returned grade is recorded, with who and when, in the course's audit history. A grade only becomes a record when it is returned. Drafts change constantly while a teacher works, and are not logged one keystroke at a time.

What students do is recorded in its own history, as completions are now: each hand-in and take-back, with its time. Staff see it on the student's work. It stays out of the audit history, which would otherwise fill with twenty entries for every assignment.

5

How it behaves

The rules below are what the server enforces. Wherever recordings already have a rule for the same situation, assignments follow it unless the card says otherwise.

Closes: the late-work date passes, or the due date does if late work is off, or the teacher closes it early. Archiving the course closes open work too; it shows as closed, not missed. More time or not required can be set for one student on any work not yet returned. Dashed: seen only by staff.

Due dates

  • Required, a whole day in institute time, and never set in the past. It prefills to a week from today.
  • Due soon means due today or in the next seven days, as it does for recordings.
  • A teacher can change it, but never to a date that has passed. Students who have not handed in are told the new date.

Like a session's listen-by date.

Late work

  • Accepted by default until a second date, a week after the due date unless the teacher changes it. Work that arrives in that week is marked late.
  • When that date passes with nothing in, the work is Missed. Without late work, that happens the day after the due date.
  • A teacher can move the late-work date later, or close it early.

Like a listen-by date, every open item has a day it closes. Unlike recordings, the day it is due and the day it closes can differ.

More time for one student

  • A teacher can give one student a later date, with a reason, before or after the date has passed, Missed work included.
  • The student sees the new date and the reason.
  • Their work is judged against their own date, so it is not late if it meets it.

Like a completion override, it needs a reason the student can see.

Handing in and taking back

  • A note, up to ten files, or both.
  • Work can be taken back until its due date, and only until the teacher returns it. After that, it is final unless the teacher asks for another try.
  • The server records the time and checks the date, as it does when someone presses play on a recording. Late is judged against the date as it stands, so moving a date later can make late work on time.

Like the deadline on recordings, which the server enforces, not the phone.

Grading and returning

  • A grade and feedback are a draft until returned, and only staff see them. Feedback is optional.
  • Return one student, or every graded one at once. Each student is told.
  • Another try: a teacher can return work asking for it to be done again, with a new date. The first attempt and its feedback stay with the second.
  • A returned grade can still be changed. The change is recorded, and the student is told only if the grade itself changed.

Like the register, which reaches students only when staff submit it.

Not required

  • A teacher can mark one student not required for one assignment, on any work not yet returned. A reason is required.
  • The student sees that it is not required, and the reason.
  • It counts nowhere: not as handed in, not as missed, not in an average. It can be undone.

Like a completion override. It is never called "excused": in this app, that word belongs to the register.

Who gets an assignment

  • Everyone enrolled when it is posted, and anyone who joins by its due date. The due date itself counts.
  • A student who joins after the due date does not get it, and it is not held against them.
  • Register marks play no part.

Unlike recordings, where a late joiner gets nothing from before they joined. An assignment not yet due is the class's current work, so a new student gets it.

Leaving and returning

  • Removing a student from a course takes its assignments off their To do.
  • Their hand-ins and grades stay in the record and the reports, flagged no longer enrolled.
  • Re-enrolling brings back the assignments not yet past their due date, and everything they handed in before.

Like leaving and returning, for recordings.

Archived courses

  • Archiving a course closes its open assignments. Nothing more can be handed in, and reminders stop. Open work shows under Archived on To do, counted neither handed in nor missed.
  • Teachers can still grade and return what was handed in. Today shows only running courses, so the archive button first says how much is still to return.
  • "Allow listening when archived" is about recordings only and does not reopen assignments.

Like recordings in an archived course with listening off.

Editing a posted assignment

  • Title, instructions, files, the due date and the late-work date can all change. The student's screen shows "Updated" with the date.
  • How it is graded cannot change once any grade has been saved.
  • Each edit is in the audit history.

Like editing a session after its recording is out.

Deleting

  • A teacher can delete an assignment no student has started, such as one posted by mistake.
  • Once any student has a draft, a hand-in, a grade or a mark on it, only an admin can delete it, after a strong confirmation, because it destroys students' work.
  • Otherwise: close it, and leave the record.

Like the rule for anything carrying listening history: disable, archive, don't delete.

Who sees what

  • A student sees their own work, grade and feedback. Never another student's, and never who else has handed in.
  • Teachers see the work of everyone in the courses they manage. Admins see everything.
  • Nobody sees a student's draft until it is handed in.

Like attendance, where staff see every mark and each student sees only their own.

Drafts of assignments

  • A draft assignment is visible to the course's teachers and admins, and to no student.
  • Posting sends it to the class and tells the students enrolled. A posted assignment does not go back to being a draft.

Like a draft recording, which reaches nobody until it is published.

7

Files

Teachers attach files to an assignment, and students attach files to their work. Both follow the same limits. The app's one hard rule about media carries over unchanged: no video, of any kind.

Teachers and students alike
AllowedDocuments: PDF, Word, PowerPoint, Excel, plain text. Pictures: JPEG, PNG, WebP, and HEIC from a phone, which the phone turns into JPEG. Audio: M4A, MP3, AAC, WAV, Ogg and Opus. Opus is the format of a WhatsApp voice note.
RefusedVideo of any kind, programs and archives. Also a HEIC picture chosen on a computer, since most browsers cannot show one; the app asks for a JPEG instead. It says why whenever it refuses a file.
How many, how bigUp to 10 files, each up to 25 MB: room for a long recitation or a scanned document. Audio a teacher attaches is limited to 15 minutes, for examples and corrections. Required listening is set by excusing students, never by attaching a class recording, which would hand it to students the register did not excuse.
PhotosShrunk on the phone to about half a megabyte before they upload. Handwriting stays readable, and upload is fast on a weak signal.
OpeningPictures show in the app. Audio plays in the app, with speed control. Documents open in the phone's or the browser's own viewer.
Who can open themThe student who handed it in, and the class's teachers and admins. Every link is issued only to someone allowed to see the file, and expires after an hour. Play links for recordings work the same way, and last twelve hours.
Saving a copyOnce a document is open in a viewer, it can be saved. That is fine for a worksheet. Class recordings are the one thing the app keeps from leaving, which is the second reason they are never attached.

What it costs

Hand-ins add about as much to storage each month as the recordings do. Recordings dominate what is downloaded, because every listen streams the whole file.

Recordings, nowAssignments, estimatedNo-cost allowance
Added each monthAbout 0.4 GB15 two-hour classesAbout 0.45 GB15 assignments × 20 students × 1.5 MB5 GB stored
Downloaded each monthAbout 16 GB600 playsAbout 1 GBeach hand-in opened once or twice100 GB

Storage builds up rather than resetting each month. Together, recordings and hand-ins pass the 5 GB allowance in the first half-year. After that each extra gigabyte costs about 2 cents a month. That comes to roughly 10 cents a month after a year, and under a dollar a month after three. Unshrunk photos would multiply the hand-in figure by about four. Video would multiply everything many times over.

The recording figures use the 32 kbps audio the cost research assumes. If Zoom delivers 128 kbps, recordings are four times larger: the allowance is passed in about ten weeks, storage costs about $1.40 a month after three years, and hand-ins become a fifth of the bucket rather than half.

9

Left out on purpose

Each of these would make the feature bigger without making it better for these classes. Each can be revisited if the teachers ask.

Video

Of any kind, as an attachment or otherwise. It is the cost cliff the product was designed to avoid.

A course grade

No overall grade per course. Teachers see an average of returned points as a working figure. If the institute wants a completion rule, say for a certificate, it can be computed later from the record already kept.

Rubrics, weighting, letter grades

Points or complete / incomplete, with written feedback, covers every kind of assignment described so far.

Conversations about the work

A note on the way in and feedback on the way back cover it. A comment thread would bring messaging, moderation and more notifications.

A message to teachers per hand-in

Twenty for one assignment. Today carries the count.

Due times

Dates only, as for recordings. A teacher who wants work before a 7 PM class sets the day before.

Quizzes and self-marking tests

A different feature, with different screens.

Group work, peer review, plagiarism checks

Nothing in these classes calls for them.

Handing in offline

Uploads need a connection. A dropped signal loses nothing already saved.

Assignments linked to a session

By design. An assignment stands on its own, whatever happened at any meeting.

Email

Push notifications only, as now.

Other people's work

No student ever sees a classmate's work, grade or whether they have handed in. There is no class feed.

10

Decisions in this draft

The first draft asked ten questions. They are answered here, along with eight more choices this draft makes. Each comes with its reason and what would change if it went the other way.

What the app calls them

Assignments.

It is the word teachers use, and it fits a reflection or a memorization as well as a worksheet; "homework" undersells the first two. The four recording messages that say "assigned" are reworded (section 8). Otherwise: "Homework" everywhere. Nothing else changes.

Where students find them

In To do, with their listening. No new tab.

A second list is a second place to forget to check. The app keeps its tab bar for places people go again and again, and "what do I owe" is one place, not two. Otherwise: an Assignments tab beside Listening, as in the first draft.

The order of To do

Open work first, the record after. Done keeps two weeks.

With two kinds of work on one list, the top of the screen should be what can still be done. Missed and archived items stay, below it. Done would otherwise grow by every assignment, every week; the class page keeps the whole record. Otherwise: Missed and Archived stay at the top, as now, and Done keeps everything.

Due dates

A date, never a time. Required, never in the past.

One rule for everything with a deadline: "Due soon" and the 7 AM reminder mean what they mean for recordings. Otherwise: a due time per assignment. Reminders and lateness then work in hours, unlike everything else in the app.

Late work

Accepted for a week after the due date, marked late. Teachers can change that date or close it early.

Homework done late is usually still worth doing, and a closing date means nothing stays open all term. Otherwise: closed at the end of the due date unless the teacher opens it.

More time for one student

Yes, with a reason the student sees.

Illness and travel happen to one student at a time. Moving the date for the whole class to help one person is the wrong tool. Otherwise: the teacher reopens the assignment for everyone.

Changing work after handing in

Take back until the due date, while it has not been returned.

A student who notices a mistake before the deadline fixes it without asking anyone. After the deadline, taking work back would make it late, so the button goes. Otherwise: handing in is final, and a teacher reopens it on request.

How work is graded

Points out of any number, or satisfactory / needs work. Feedback is optional.

That covers memorization, reflections and worksheets alike. "Complete" is already the word for finished listening, so the second scale avoids it. Letter grades, rubrics and weighting add policy the institute has not asked for. Otherwise: satisfactory / needs work only, which removes averages from the reports.

When a student sees the grade

When the teacher returns it, together or one at a time.

The teacher chooses the moment. Grading moves on with Next and offers to return everything at the end, so a class gets its work back together. Return now is there for a teacher who returns work as it arrives. Otherwise: visible as soon as it is typed. Typos and half-finished feedback would reach students.

Another try

In the first build, as a way of returning work.

Without it, "needs work" has nowhere to go. The first attempt and its feedback stay with the second. Otherwise: a student hands in again only if the teacher reopens the assignment.

Letting one student off one assignment

"Not required", with a reason the student sees.

The same shape as a listening override, reason included. It is never called "excused", which means one thing in this app: the register mark. Otherwise: no exemptions. Such students would show as past due, then missed.

Who receives an assignment

Everyone enrolled, and anyone who joins by its due date.

Assignments are independent of attendance, as you set out. Joining after the date gets you nothing to answer for. Otherwise: chosen students only, which part 3 adds for make-up work.

Messages

Four for students under three switches, none for teachers.

Students need to know when work appears, when it moves, when it is due and when it comes back. Teachers already open Today, and a message per hand-in would be noise. Otherwise: a 7 AM summary of work to grade for teachers, with its own switch.

Grading on an admin's Today

Only for courses they manage by name.

An admin's Today covers every running course. Grading for all of them would bury the registers that keep students from their recordings. Otherwise: every course's grading, for every admin.

Files

Documents, pictures and audio; 10 files of 25 MB; a teacher's audio at most 15 minutes; photos shrunk; no video.

Enough for a scanned worksheet or a long recitation, and close to the no-cost allowance (section 7). The 15-minute limit keeps class recordings from being handed out as files, which would go around the register. Otherwise: larger limits cost little; video is the one thing that would cost a lot.

A camera button

On phones, in the app and the browser.

Handwritten Arabic will be much of what these classes hand in, and a photo is how it gets here. Otherwise: students photograph first, then pick the file. One more step for every hand-in.

A grade for the course

None.

There is no institute rule to compute it from, and a number the app invents would be read as official. Otherwise: a percentage per course, shown to staff and students, with a rule written down first.

Deleting an assignment

Teachers, until a student starts it; then admins only.

A mistake can be undone, but students' work cannot be destroyed casually. That is the app's rule for anything carrying history. Otherwise: admins only, always, which makes a typo in a fresh post an admin's job.

Build order

Parts 1 and 2 are the feature shown on this page. Part 3 is a list to come back to once teachers have used it. Each part ships to the website and Android together, with its manual pages and a pass on a real device.

Part 1 · the loop

Post, hand in, grade, return

  • Writing and posting: formatted instructions, files, camera, drafts, editing, the late-work date, deleting.
  • To do with both kinds of work; the assignment screen; hand in and take back.
  • Grading on a phone and a laptop; return together or one at a time; another try; grading work done on paper; more time for one student; not required.
  • Today's To grade group; the course page; the assignment page; both class pages.
  • Four student messages, and taps that open the assignment; audit entries; the hand-in history.
  • The reworded recording messages; the product brief; the manual.

Ships when a class could use it from post to return, on both surfaces.

Part 2 · the record

Reports and the way back

  • The Grades report, and the gradebook on wide screens.
  • Workbook sheets for courses and students.
  • A Record button beside Take a photo, so a recitation needs no other app.
  • Files on feedback, such as a voice note on a recitation.
  • Copy an assignment from another course, for the next cohort.

Ships when the term's records can be exported.

Part 3 · if asked

Time-savers

  • Posting on a chosen date, at 7 AM.
  • An assignment for chosen students only, such as make-up work.
  • Posting one assignment to several sections of a course at once.

Each one on its own, when a teacher asks for it.

For the developer

How part 1 fits the code as it stands at v0.7.1. Most of it reuses patterns this repository or the Kanban app already has; work with no precedent is marked new.

Records, rules, server, files, messages, tests Tap to open

Names

assignments is taken: it is the listening grant (AssignmentDoc, assignmentsFanout.ts, its rules block, the brief's own section). The new records are coursework and submissions, after the Google Classroom API. Only the interface says "assignment".

Records

coursework/{id}                      staff write via callables
  courseId cohortId title instructions(md) files[]
  dueDate lateUntil|null grading{points,outOf|scale}
  status(draft|posted) postedAt postedBy updatedAt
submissions/{uid}_{courseworkId}     one per student, fanned out
  studentUid courseworkId courseId cohortId active
  dueDate lateUntil   the student's own, after more time
  state(open|handedIn|returned) attempt handedInAt
  note files[] notRequired{reason}? moreTime{reason}?
  returned{grade feedback files[] at seq retry}  server only
drafts/{uid}_{courseworkId}          the student's alone
  studentUid courseworkId courseId note files[]
grades/{uid}_{courseworkId}          staff only
  studentUid courseworkId courseId grade feedback
  gradedBy gradedAt
submissionEvents/{auto}              append-only, server

Who reads what

  • Students list submissions by studentUid, then get each posted coursework, gated on holding an active submission. This mirrors recordings gated on a grant.
  • The grade reaches the student only as submissions.returned, copied when the work is returned, like the attendance projection. grades/ is never student-readable; Firestore has no field-level security.
  • Staff queries pin courseId (==, or in up to QUEUE_SCOPE), which is why grades/ and drafts/ carry it. The admin arm comes first. drafts/ has no staff arm at all.
  • Students write only their own drafts/ note, shape-checked. Everything else goes through callables.

Server

  • auditedCall: createCoursework, updateCoursework, postCoursework, deleteCoursework, add/removeCourseworkFile, returnWork (another try included), giveMoreTime, setNotRequired, and saveGrade. saveGrade sets audit.noop while the grade is an unreturned draft, so only changes to a returned grade are logged. One ACTION_LABELS entry per action, combined where an action goes both ways, as "Archived/unarchived cohort" is.
  • reportedCall: handIn, takeBack, add/removeDraftFile, getFileUrl (one-hour V4 signed URL). handIn and takeBack append submissionEvents.
  • Every requireCourseScope call site needs a row in the callableScope test, reportedCalls included, such as the staff path of getFileUrl.
  • deleteCoursework is gated like requireDeleteRights. It probes submissionEvents, drafts and grades for any student work, as hasRecordingHistory does, rather than reading current state.
  • The fan-out reconciles on posting, on enrolment (due date today or later) and on unenrolment (active:false), beside reconcileCourseAssignments.

Dates

  • dueDate and lateUntil are YYYY-MM-DD in INSTITUTE_TIMEZONE, validated as a session's due date is (never written in the past). The due date prefills to today plus DEFAULT_DUE_DAYS, and lateUntil to the due date plus seven.
  • handIn refuses when the course is not effectiveActive, or today is past the student's lateUntil (their dueDate when late work is off). takeBack refuses after the student's dueDate or once returned.
  • Late is derived from handedInAt against the student's current dueDate and never stored, so moving a date re-judges it. The rules never compare dates.
  • To do reuses dueBucket, with a pastDue bucket for open coursework. Done sorts by when the item was finished.

Storage

coursework/{courseworkId}/{fileId}       any active staff
work/{uid}/{courseworkId}/{fileId}       that uid only
feedback/{courseworkId}/{uid}/{fileId}   any active staff (part 2)
  • Write-once, unreadable, at most 25 MB. These rules do not read Firestore, so course scope, enrolment, open state and the ten-file cap are enforced when a callable finalizes the file. The accepted residual is the Kanban app's: an active account can write unreadable, billable bytes that nothing links to. Students get their first Storage write path here.
  • Finalizing reads the size from the object and re-sanitizes the declared type. Nothing inspects the bytes, so a video declared as audio still stops at 25 MB. A teacher's 15-minute audio limit uses the duration the device measures, as manual upload does: a guard, not a proof.
  • new Mirroring a content-type allowlist into the rules, with a test; the precedents mirror only the size. A sweeper for abandoned uploads follows the Kanban app's ATTACHMENT_UPLOAD_STALE_MS.

Messages

  • assignmentPosted is sent by postCoursework, and by updateCoursework when the date moves, not by a trigger on submissions, so joiners and re-enrolments get no message. Target {id}_{dueDate}.
  • handInLastDay comes from onMorning, target {id}_{dueDate}. workReturned targets {id}_{returned.seq}, so another return is not swallowed by notifyOnce.
  • Each kind goes into NOTIFICATION_KINDS (PREF_KEYS follows), STUDENT_KINDS, NOTIFICATION_LABEL, NOTIFICATION_DESCRIPTION, NotificationPrefsDoc, the rules whitelist and the literal list in notificationPrefKeys.test.ts. Rename the channel; keep the id sabeel-alerts.
  • new Opening the assignment from a tap. The message carries the route, followed by a response listener on Android and the service worker's click handler on the web. Today a tap only brings the app forward.

Today and the screens

  • A grading kind, last in KIND_ORDER, never blocking. Its scope is the courses whose managerUids hold the reader, for admins too, rather than the queue's usual admin scope. One query on submissions (handed in) and one on grades, each with a useListenerFailed label.
  • New routes: Coursework, CourseworkEdit, Grade, Grades (staff); Work (student). Each wrapper is keyed on its subject.
  • StudentLedgerScreen and StudentAttendanceScreen each gain a Segmented.

Editor and pickers

  • Port the Kanban app's editor: RichEditor.web.tsx (Lexical) and RichEditor.tsx (react-native-enriched-html), with RichToolbar, LinkSheet, the shared RichText renderer, and richtext.ts / richtextHtml.ts behind isSafeHref and the round-trip fuzz. Strip the @mentions. RichEditor.tsx needs react-native-reanimated, a new native dependency to agree first. Measure the web bundle and the APK before and after.
  • Right-to-left: the editor and the renderer must take paragraph direction from the first strong character, on both platforms. The Kanban app has no right-to-left handling, so this is new and gets a test.
  • filePicker grows documents, several files, images and the camera (expo-image-picker, as in the Kanban app). new Shrinking photos and turning HEIC into JPEG (expo-image-manipulator), and the camera in a phone's browser. Only Safari decodes HEIC, so elsewhere a HEIC is refused with a request for a JPEG.

Reports

  • new One source for the assignment counts, courseworkStats, read by the screens, the Grades report and both workbooks. Listening is counted twice today, by rollup for the screens and courseStudentStats for the workbook; assignments should not repeat that.
  • New sheets in workbooks.ts; new terms in WORKBOOK_DEFINITIONS. Manual §2.13 must match them word for word (workbookDefinitions.test.ts).

Tests that state the promise

  • "A student cannot see a grade before it is returned": read grades/ and the submission as the student, before and after the return.
  • "A refused delete destroys nothing": drive the core as a manager on coursework that has a draft but no hand-in, then assert the draft, its files and the coursework remain.
  • "Moving a date re-judges late": hand in after the due date, give that student more time, and assert the work is no longer late.
  • "Joining after the due date owes nothing": enrol, then assert no submission exists and the To do bucket is empty.
  • "No staff arm on drafts": the rules suite, flipped to if true once, to show it can fail.
  • Plus the sweep: the new screens in the staff, manager and student tours, with seed coursework in every state. Plus the composite-index check on each new query.

Device pass

  • Camera capture and resize; picking each allowed type; a dropped connection mid-upload.
  • Arabic in the editor and the renderer on Android.
  • Audio attachments playing, with speed and seek.
  • Notification taps opening the assignment; the mini-player above To do.
